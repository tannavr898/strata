A probe is only useful if its numbers can be trusted like a lab's. Measure the same samples both ways, enter the pairs, and Strata fits the straight line that maps one onto the other — then applies it to readings that don't have a calibrated value yet.

Readings already calibrated are left alone. Re-deriving them under a new fit would quietly change numbers you may have already acted on.

At least three pairs. Two points always fit a line perfectly, which would report a flawless R² that means nothing.

What cannot be calibrated, and why

These measurements have no laboratory equivalent to map onto, so Strata does not offer a calibration for them. That is a real limit, not a missing feature: inventing a mapping to a scale that does not exist would produce official-looking numbers with nothing behind them.
